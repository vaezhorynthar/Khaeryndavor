# Khaeryndavor

> The coin had three faces. The hand that spent it remembered only two.

## The Market beneath the Bronze Rain

Khaeryndavor opens in a square where rain falls as small bronze circles. They strike the pavement without a sound and vanish before anyone can gather them. Each leaves a brief reflection of a stall standing somewhere beyond the square.

Merchants arrive carrying empty trays. They arrange their wares by naming what is absent: a summer without shadows, a footstep without a traveler, the warmth left inside a glove. Visitors recognize these offerings even when they have never lost them.

At the center stands a moneychanger's table. Upon it lies a coin with three faces, each visible from a different direction. None bears a portrait. One shows a closed eye, another a folded wing, and the third a hand holding a smaller coin.

## The Buyer of an Unfallen Feather

A buyer entered the square carrying a feather that had never touched the ground. It hovered above his palm, casting the shadow of a long scaled neck. He asked what price the moneychanger would offer.

The moneychanger placed the three-faced coin beneath the feather. Its third face showed the buyer's hand, now empty. The feather lowered until it rested upon the image instead of the metal.

The buyer accepted the coin. Every merchant turned away. Bronze rain began falling inside the reflection of a stall, though the square itself remained dry.

Before leaving, he asked whether he had sold the feather or the distance beneath it. The moneychanger returned an empty tray and told him to carry it level until the shadow found its owner.

By dawn, the tray had acquired the weight of a creature sleeping with its head beyond the rim.

## The Ledger of Unpaid Weather

The moneychanger keeps a ledger bound in thin bronze. Its pages describe weather that has been promised but not delivered. A storm belongs to a village whose name has faded. A clear night belongs to someone who cannot remember sleeping.

One entry concerns a snowfall inside a room without windows. Its price is recorded as the sound of a wing closing. Its witness is a small hand drawn in place of a name.

A visitor attempted to settle that entry with a bell. The moneychanger weighed it against an empty tray. The tray descended, and the bell's reflection appeared inside the bronze binding, ringing over a field covered in snow.

The visitor kept the bell. Its voice no longer reached anyone inside a room. Outdoors, it sounded like a hand knocking upon a closed window.

The entry remained unpaid, but its drawn witness began wearing a glove.

## The Creature beneath the Stalls

During the market's quietest hour, the pavement becomes transparent. Beneath it lies a pale dragon curled around a square much smaller than the one above. Its scales bear tiny circles resembling coins viewed edgewise.

The smaller square contains a table and an empty tray. Sometimes a feather can be seen resting upon the tray. Sometimes the tray holds a glove with a bell hidden inside its fingers.

The dragon opens its wings without moving its body. Each wing reveals another market, its merchants looking upward through transparent ground. Visitors above glimpse themselves among those merchants, offering things they have not yet lost.

The moneychanger covers the central table until the pavement turns opaque. He never covers the coin. Its third face remains warm long after the other two become cold.

## The Inscription along the Rim

**Khaer — ynda — vor — eulth.**

These sounds appear around the coin when it is balanced upon an empty tray. Its letters change shape whenever the bronze rain reaches the square, but the moneychanger pronounces them without hesitation.

A scholar tried to record their meaning. Her notes described three hands, a feather, and a sleeping creature beneath a market. They contained no words corresponding to the inscription.

She turned the coin toward its third face. The small hand in the image held a book open to her notes. Behind it stood a merchant offering an unwritten page.

The scholar exchanged her explanation for that page. Its blank surface reflected the square on a day when every stall was closed. Upon the moneychanger's table lay a coin with a single face, showing an eye that had begun to open.

## A Bargain Spoken without Witnesses

> Which face belongs to the hand that spends the coin?
>
> The one that remembers neither side.
>
> What does the feather weigh above the tray?
>
> The sleep of the neck that casts its shadow.
>
> When will the promised snow reach its room?
>
> When the glove returns the voice it borrowed from the bell.

The buyer heard these answers while carrying his tray through an empty street. No speaker followed him. The coin remained in his pocket, showing three faces whenever he felt it with a closed hand.

He returned at the next market. His tray was empty again, but its reflection held a feather upon a pale scaled neck.

## The Price after the Rain

The bronze circles continue falling and vanishing. The merchants keep their empty trays level, and the moneychanger preserves the entry concerning snow inside a windowless room.

Sometimes the central coin rings without being struck. Sometimes a visitor leaves with a glove whose fingers move before the hand inside them does.

Khaeryndavor waits between the offered absence and the accepted weather. Beneath its pavement, a sleeping creature turns toward a smaller square, where a hand is learning which face of a coin can remember the rain.
